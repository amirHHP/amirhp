When Márquez, in *Love in the Time of Cholera*, put Florentino in a romantic purgatory opposite Fermina for more than half a century, he perhaps never knew the world would change so much that his lover’s running around would become this incomprehensible. Florentino lived in longing, hoping that by chance he would suddenly meet his beloved or see a sign of her. He wrote letters, traveled, asked others about her, and followed her by every path there was. Now cyberspace has tied every relationship together.

![img](http://www.touchgraph.com/assets/images/TouchGraph%20Hashable%20SXSW.png)

In analyzing a social network, one of the most important traits to look at is that members should be seen not merely as individuals but as nodes of a social graph. So each person is a bridge that takes you to someone else. With that in mind, when you so-called become friends with someone in virtual space (never mind how much virtual space has also changed the meaning of friend), you are in truth starting a relationship not only with them but with the thousands of people connected to them. Now suppose, by bad luck, you want to cut your ties with someone. In that case you have to get rid not only of them but of their friends, and their friends’ friends, and in general of the huge social graph that connects them to thousands of others. If you do not, after deleting your connection with that person, because of the ties that formed between you and their friends, they will appear in front of your eyes every time as a friend suggestion.

And that is only part of it. The number of social networks and their inner entanglement and connections have made things more complicated still. If you added the person on Facebook, you see their photos and friends in Instagram’s suggestion tab. If you once emailed them, every time you enter Google Plus it suggests you add them to your circles. If their number is in your phone, Swarm says follow them so you know their physical location at every moment. Worst of all, if you added them on Google Plus, every time you search a phrase they appear, unwanted, among your results.

And all of this is on the condition that you first withstand the temptation to check their profiles on different networks. Márquez never knew that one day cutting human ties would become this impossible.
