A fairly long time has passed since the last post. In this one I want to write about the Sepas project, what happened to it, and the decisions that were made. In short, Sepas is a health app that will be designed in a particular way, with a particular user experience. With this software you can easily, without stress and pressure, lose, control, or gain weight.

A few months after the Sepas idea came first at Lean Startup Machine, I started knocking on doors to make it real. Suddenly I remembered a high-school friend I had not seen in a long time, and from Facebook I knew he worked in Android development. I told him the idea, visited their small company a few times, and saw they were barely doing their own work. They even asked me, if I knew an Android developer, to introduce them.

Later a friend introduced someone else. The problem was that he was in another city, and the few times he came to Tehran I never managed a proper meeting, and the talks never went beyond phone calls. So I decided not to go after people in another city anymore.

A few weeks passed until, when I went to Zanjan and Zicanf for a report, in that interesting space I met an Android developer. Over those two days we talked and turned the idea over, but in the end he said he would not have time to work on it for a few months, and then he even said the company he worked at had been looking for someone, and I could go there if I wanted. He said it did not even matter whether I had ability and experience; I could learn Android in that same company. In the end that acquaintance also led nowhere. A few weeks after universities started I realized I had neglected some of the most important people who could help. So I found a classmate I knew had done programming and shared the project with him. Among the three previous talks, this time I heard the most logical and professional words. He looked at my idea not as a mere programmer but as a software engineer or even a businessman, challenged it, and found its most important flaws. With his help we looked at many apps, Iranian and not. He insisted the idea was not new and that many similar ones already existed, but in the end I convinced him that the user experience I had in mind matched none of the existing software and could even sell well abroad. Still, there was a large problem: they were not willing to implement a product with no reliable business plan for free. In my view a startup, at birth, never knows how it will reach profit, but I also understood that they would not want to put in hours with no backing for a program that may or may not sell. At first I thought we could add a new feature and, in return, get help from the municipality for funding. Apparently in that case the project would not stay ours; it would go under the municipality’s name. Later he said he could arrange a chance to talk with one of the operators, but after some thought I decided I did not want my service lost among the colorful, mass operator offerings that come and go every year. I want to grow it myself and raise money for it to stay alive.

## The future is bright

Implementation of the app is supposed to start soon. I have more or less convinced the team that through Donait and ads at sports clubs we can raise starting capital until we find a more reliable business model. But for the start of the road I need a little capital, at least to convince the team that the project will go somewhere. So if you want to birth a project with your own hands and your own money, help Sepas. Our project is [on Donait](https://sepas.2nate.com/). From 2,000 tomans to 500,000, whatever you give is encouraging and useful for us.
