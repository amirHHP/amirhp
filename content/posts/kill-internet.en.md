After all this time, without any preamble, I want to invite you to read an article titled “One bridge higher in 1391” by Arash Barahmand, a well-known ICT journalist. The note was published on the 20th of Esfand in Asr-e Ertebat weekly, page 2. A hot, fiery note that was a little strange to see in a newspaper.

I guessed a lot of people missed it, and because the online version was also in poor shape, I quote part of it here with my own comments.

> 1390 for the country’s information technology **was the year of a complete shift of IT stewardship to governmental levels**, and every sign points to the government’s role falling into a coma. For such a reading of the ruling conditions there are at least three fully tangible reasons:
>
> The first reason is political: the rise of freedom-seeking movements across Middle Eastern governments, from Egypt to Tunisia, Qatar, and even Jordan, almost all of them relying on technology tools and especially social networks, made Iranian policymakers notice this settling truth: technology, though it does not itself produce social and political unrest, plays a key role in spreading and speeding it, and that truth is not national or regional but global. What happened in the British unrest and the Occupy Wall Street movement is also a borderless embodiment of issues of this kind. The result is that the Islamic Republic of Iran, depending on its local conditions and outer challenges, tried to understand and digest this forming immaterial superpower. Sometimes mobile phones could also be a weapon.

The result of this fear, which had existed for a long time and which I had also pointed to many times in my own writing, this time, after the national-internet plan was keyed and the internet was made more expensive in a meaningful move only a few days after the parliamentary election, showed itself in the formation of the Supreme Council of Cyberspace and then the National Center of Cyberspace. The result of that work is more control of the internet and paving the way for the “clean internet” plan, or as they call it, the internal information network.

> … more than two years after Telecom was sold, it is clearer than ever that of the ICT ministry, in the absence of the telecom apparatus (which includes 30 influential provincial companies, MCI, and the holding company), only a hollow shell remains … the image of this unresolved position of the ministry went as far as a step from dissolution and merger, to prove that from now on **power in the country’s ICT pyramid is determined not by closeness to the government but by closeness to the ruling system.** The new role that governing bodies such as IRIB also seek can be read on the same bed.

Once more we look at the decree forming the Supreme Council of Cyberspace. The presence of roles such as the IRGC commander, the intelligence minister, the police commander, and the head of IRIB shows the aims of this council, which are more control of the internet. The role of the ICT ministry as the only related body is worth pausing on. Whether the aim is really a military, choking space, or a healthy environment.

> Third: it was in this same year that after a decade of effort by academic, private, guild, and even government hands to remove threat from the mindset ruling the country’s IT, in dealing with people in this field, especially in areas such as internet telephony, broadband providers, and even mobile value-added services, security tools were used, and it went beyond license cancellation, sealing the workplace, and a legal warning… new commercial-guild principles, in a word a new geography, came to rule this field, which even in the first years of the internet and officials’ suspicion had no similar. In these same years the internet has been called equivalent to pornography, forbidden trade, or contrary to security, which has made the already difficult ICT trade under sanctions and recession look even more unpleasant than it is.

And in the end it is clear that the work has gone beyond the government and lower officials, and this space has created so much fear that the ruler himself is personally after killing it. But that death looks like an impossible wish.
