Organizations usually reach a point that looks like a point of no return. At that point the core of the organization “does not know that it does not know,” and it keeps going downhill with real force. The last days of these places, I think, have a recognizable shape.

1. Unworthy people rise.
People with no merit, who would have no place in any other company, take the highest seats and plant their thinking there with ease.

2. There is no clear path to the decision-makers.
The few capable people who are left have no idea how to grow, how to join management, how to become decision-makers. The few who still want change do not know where to start.

3. Only a particular group of loyalists remain.
The loyalists are a special set. They are loyal all the way through. They have no critique, they approve of the current structure, or if they do critique, they do it in a way that keeps the main structure intact.

4. The decision-makers do not stand by their word.
The inner circle says one thing every day and, before the week is out, forgets it, pretends to forget it, or knowingly walks it back. Slowly their word loses all credit. Nobody counts on their promises, not even their own people. Everyone knows that if so-and-so said something, it does not really matter.

5. Everyone can see the incompetence of the person at the top, and they talk about it morning, noon, and night.
The whispering inside the organization has reached its peak. A kind of helplessness. As if nobody cares about the actual work anymore and would rather wait for the appointed hour. Every day they gossip about what happened that day, and the time spent whispering has overtaken the time spent working.

6. HR worries about the CEO instead of the people.
HR has drifted toward the CEO’s concerns. The smallest former comforts are cut in the name of saving money. Promotions and raises become harder and more opaque.

7. You do not know how the money is spent.
Every day you see strange expenses you cannot explain. One day a few new sofas, another day a full office redesign — while you have had no raise for a year.

8. Even the good things happen without reason or logic.
If the company wins something, nobody knows why. Everyone wonders how that award arrived, or why we threw that party. Nobody knows how the wins actually happened, and slowly nobody believes them. They stop feeling like a shared achievement and start belonging to a private circle.

9. This looks like the last blow: even the loyal group no longer does their unimportant work properly. A kind of numbness and lack of motive. Maybe they are tired, or they no longer believe the work does any good.

We have all lived through days like these. Apparently the organization has two roads left. Either the CEO fires a wide band of people and buys a few months for new minds to enter, or the management structure is changed entirely. Changed meaning no trace of the old people and the old outlook remains in the fabric of the place.

We probably know well that neither of these happens, and a few months after the collapse nobody remembers that such an organization ever existed.
