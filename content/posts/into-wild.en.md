When I watched [Into the Wild](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0758758/) I found it like a strange, lost experience I had to dive into as soon as I could.

A boy who one day leaves everything he has to return to his own origin. Before he even reaches university, with nothing, he goes into nature and lives the rest of his life there, apart from society.

For me, who sees nature as the real mother of humans, that journey is a return to human origin. Nature and solitude let us turn back to ourselves and look there for what has probably been lost in ordinary modern life.

These days, having found the blog [Saboktar](http://saboktar.com/) and following its three writers on Instagram, I thought of Into the Wild again, and I longed to dive into unplanned events, in a confrontation with nature. Saboktar is the work of three women who have lived travel and a fluid life much more simply and freely than we do. With no daily plan, not much money, mostly with passing cars and other people’s houses, they have taken many trips. They tickled the sleeping giant in me again: that despite every worry, six months from now I should try a new kind of travel.

P.S. My second backpacking trip also failed. I did not like the wandering and the not-knowing. I preferred my mind to be free of “where do I sleep tonight” or “how do I get back,” so I could think about “more important” things. Sometimes I think I was maybe made for this machine life, a time when superiority is summarized in how many books we read and how much we worked in a day.

P.S. 2: I even concluded that Saboktar does not write honestly. It tries to describe travel as sweeter than it really is.
