Many ideas, however clearly they are defined and standardized, take on different meanings in practice and in different places. Product management is one of those roles that is defined differently everywhere in the world. My years of working as a product manager in different companies, plus the reading I did in that time, gave me a deeper sense of where this role sits in Iran.

## What is product management?

The product manager role appeared because companies shipped many products, and because the CEO could not focus on all of them, each line got its own product manager. That is why a product manager is treated as a mini-CEO: they own that product from end to end.

In general, a product manager’s authority over that product is proportional to a CEO’s authority over the whole company. In Iran, though, it has wrongly taken hold that a product manager is someone who “follows up” on the organization’s requests. The CEO, the board, and other managers announce their product ideas and expect the product manager to set a timeline. In truth they are treated as a “project manager” who says whether the ideas are technically possible and when they will be delivered.

## What is the difference between a product manager and a product owner?

Product manager is a floating role. Its definition and duties change from organization to organization — like an office manager who in one place also handles building work and in another is responsible for people and equipment. A product owner, by contrast, is a standard role with international definitions. Scrum spells out in detail what is in scope and what is not.

## What skills do you need for product management?

Product management is a multi-sided role. The wider your knowledge, the more useful you can be. It is not a craft you can pick up in a year or two with no prior experience, though in Iran many people reach the title at the start of their career or with little experience. Because the role is meant to drive the organization, an inexperienced product manager follows the organization instead of giving it the direction it needs.

T-shaped people usually make better product managers: they know a little about many domains and are deep in one. They work better than I-shaped people who are specialists in a single area.

## How do you become a product manager?

I suggest at least five years in a specific industry, in different roles. Sometimes a product manager was a good programmer who also became interested in business and marketing. Sometimes they were a good “business person” who learned how to talk to engineers. To be effective you need real experience in business development, marketing, PR, content, branding, HR, product development, and programming.

Once you start as a PM or PO, your depth and long view are what save the team from crises and wrong turns. Soft skills and communication are also one of your strongest cards. People management sits with the Scrum Master, but your soft skills help you work better with stakeholders and engineering.

## Do you need a specific degree or course?

There are many product courses online, but they only make you a product specialist. To become a good product manager you have to be broad and experienced.

Certificates in Scrum and project management may help a little by giving you a more organized mind and a standard way to use what you know. Scrum.org has PSPO for product ownership and PAL for agile leadership.

## How much does a product manager earn?

The role is strategic and high-stakes. A mistake can kill the organization or the product line, so it is highly responsible and, by nature, better paid. Pay in Iran is not transparent, so the range is wide. As a PO in good organizations you might make 5 to 9 million tomans; as a PM (assuming the product manager is more of a decision-maker, owning strategy and the team) perhaps 10 to 20 million. The spread comes from how floating the role is.

These figures are from when the piece was written.

## When is the right time to hire a product manager?

If you run a startup, my personal, experience-based view is that after raising more than one billion tomans you need to set the company structure properly. The trap is hiring a product manager for that. At that point you probably need a product-owner model more, and it is cheaper and more logical once you have more than one product, or when you want to work on several products with more focus and clarity.

Hiring a product owner helps you as a founder spend more time on growth, people, structure, investors, new capital, partners, networking, preparing to scale, key metrics, and hiring talent. It is your choice which founder duties you hand off. If your core value is the software, put more into the product team; you may even need a part-time Scrum Master. If the business needs a strong sales or operations team, focus somewhere else.

## Why does every founder who fails become a product manager?

The question, asked on Instagram, is not always true, but the reason is this: after a failed startup, the closest role to being a founder is product management. As a founder you do everything, and everything comes from the needs of the business. That is exactly what a product manager does for one product inside an organization. If you want to keep an entrepreneurial spirit after a failed company, product management is probably your best seat.

## What should a product manager do about people who delete the app or never use it?

One of the most important jobs is knowing the sales funnel: how the user journey runs, and the four steps of Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral, watching the metrics daily and changing the product to improve them. So a product manager needs a real relationship with marketing and with the inbound channels.

The job is to inspect the funnel and find where the product is breaking it. Alongside that, the product manager has to find the right persona and suggest the best acquisition channels so the intended audience actually enters the product.

## What should I have studied?

There is almost no specific degree for this role. You might have studied IT or software and then worked in tourism for years. You might have studied tourism and learned software or business. You might have studied management, economics, or business and then become skilled in a particular market.

## Is product management like project management?

Product management has nothing to do with project management. A product manager is not supposed to, and cannot, give a reliable estimate of when the project becomes profitable, or when the software is ready to grow. They are good at reading the market and changing strategy in time to raise the odds of success.
