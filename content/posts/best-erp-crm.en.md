Thirty years ago, when a PhD student at MIT grew tired of the monopoly on programming and decided to stand against that scientific prison, the idea of “free software” was born. These programs grew at a dizzying rate and helped the progress of programming a great deal. Organizations, like every other institution that depends on software, were caught in that wave and took a lot of open-source software inside. Today, besides programs backed by large companies, there are others that chose to release their software as open source, both to help organizational technology and to take another step out from under proprietary software.![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a6/Gnucash_2_screenshot.png)

For finance software aimed at small businesses, and even personal use, there are very strong examples. They give you full graphical reports, and you can even do scheduled transactions. GnuCash is one of the most active and oldest free accounting programs, and it is still developing well. On SourceForge it has the most downloads among accounting software. Its latest version shipped two months ago.![](https://cdn.kde.org/screenshots/kmymoney/kmymoney.png)

Those two are old hands in this field, but Manager is a newer, different example. It comes in desktop, server, and cloud versions. Manager has a fully modern, flat interface, and on the cloud version’s page you can try the features.

When you enter the program, the first page shows a summary of your system. You can get an overall picture from different sections and even customize those parts. You can create users and different businesses and manage them at the same time. Another feature is Persian: 55 percent of it has been translated so far, and every user can help translate this accounting software. That attention to participation and easy collaboration is the spirit of most open-source projects: that everyone can play a part in improving a piece of the world. Making the world of zeros and ones better was one of Richard Stallman’s central concerns as the founder of software freedom.

These programs can cover small businesses, but mid-size organizations need tools beyond them. Larger organizations need programs to manage many people, resources, and customers. Software in this group is more comprehensive and is often built on different management ideas and philosophies.

The customer, as the most vital capital of a commercial organization, sits under ideas like E-CRM, a philosophy for keeping and expanding relationships and serving current and future customers better. Customer-relationship software is, for example, a database of an organization’s customers that management and sales or support staff use to match products to needs. Sales uses that information to contact the customer and offer goods and services. The same information is very valuable for senior managers making large decisions. After a sale, when a customer calls, these programs are used to retrieve and store the customer’s information. By serving faster and more efficiently, and by keeping the data in one place, the company tries to cut costs and attract new customers.

### Human resource management (HRM)

One of a company’s first needs is to know its people well and place them in the right layer of the organization. An organization also needs to replace people in time and keep trying to attract new ones.

### Why does using free software matter in organizations?

The real power of free software is in the license it gives others. Under these licenses anyone can, for any purpose, “run” the code, “study” it freely, “change” it, and “publish” it again under the same license. Those four freedoms have results that also help an organization. First, because of the freedom to study, a development team can inspect open-source code and make sure it is sound and not malicious. That keeps spyware with a back door out of critical organizations. Open-source programs, because the code is available and a large community sits behind them, usually have their bugs found quickly and fixed before they are exploited. Second, most open-source software is free in the sense that they do not charge for the code. Companies that ship open source make money in other ways, such as support. So using it in an organization cuts costs. Third, because of the third and fourth freedoms you can edit an open-source program and republish it with only programming skill. Your organization can buy it or take it for free and then change it to fit its needs. Fourth, in proprietary software, when a company drops support, the program is finished, because if something breaks nobody takes responsibility. That has happened many times, even on large projects. In open source, even if the original company stops, other developers continue and keep the program alive. That is what happened with MySQL and OpenOffice after they were bought: distrust of Oracle’s policies on open source made developers fork, and those forks are now stronger than the originals. Fifth, open-source software is good at localization and usually supports it strongly. Helping to Persianize these programs makes them usable for staff with little training, and that cuts finished costs even more.

Supply-chain management (SCM) covers all of an organization’s movement and storage of raw materials, work in progress, and finished goods from start to consumption. With it, inputs and outputs are controlled, and the product is tracked from the moment it enters as raw material through production and out as a finished good. It is about coordinating material flow between several organizations and inside each one. To do that you need technologies and tools to track materials from origin to destination and to record information at each step. There are close to twenty open-source programs in accounting and organizational work. A few are much more active than the rest and have a real hold on this market.

### Open ERP

This ERP is one of the most complete organizational programs, written in Python and shipped as client, server, and web. One feature is a 15-day trial of the web version. Go to openerp.com, fill in the form, and enter. The site also works on small screens, so you can use the ERP on a phone. When you enter your trial account you see a list of management solutions designed as modules; you can install whichever you need with one click. That way all the tools look and behave alike, and you buy only the parts you need. You do not pay for a full suite; you buy only the tests you want.

### FrontAccounting

FrontAccounting is another program, more suited to small businesses than large ones. A demo is also available online from the maker’s site. A third-party partner has shipped a mobile version since 2011, so you can do organizational work from a phone. The environment is simple. There are ready tabs at the top; each tab takes you to a separate section where you pick a subsection. Doing things in parallel is hard, because each time you have to walk those steps to get to the part you want.

### Openbravo

The Spanish company’s office is in Barcelona, and it has two web products in its basket: a point of sale and an ERP. Its products have three times won InfoWorld’s best open-source software award from that 35-year-old online outlet. One strength is a 30-day online demo; you can go to the site and look at the features. The latest version is from less than a month ago, in a professional edition and a free retail edition. After the steps you can download the file and run it in a virtualization tool in a separate virtual environment. Openbravo’s interface is much simpler and, from one view, older than the others. When you enter, the first page shows an overall report from different sections. You can add various widgets and have full stats without moving between sections. In the bar at the top there is a floating Applications menu that groups organizational software into many precise subgroups. That access, plus opening programs in parallel in separate tabs, makes the work much faster. Every section also has several views: you can see documents as a list in half the page and, by clicking one, see it as a form in the other half without changing the page.

### ADempiere

ADempiere is an Italian project written in Java, in development and use since September 2006. Unlike the others it is more community-driven, and it used to be one of SourceForge’s most popular programs. A fair number of manuals and histories have been written for it. Unfortunately the company has not used marketing principles to introduce the product; the site is very simple and designed as a wiki. Unlike its rivals, though, the program is completely free. You can download and install it at no cost.

### Apache OFBiz

OFBiz is another organizational program, this time with a backer as large as the Apache Software Foundation, and its advantage over the others is that well-known identity. Unfortunately Apache has not given the product a good site, and there is not even a preview. OFBiz does not look like a good final solution, and as the site says, if you want a painless product you should stay away. If you want to use the latest technologies and help an open-source project, this is exactly the place you were looking for. To run it you download a hundred-megabyte file from the site and start it from the command line. After that it is installed on localhost and you can enter through a browser.
