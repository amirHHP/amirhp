The gas mask is groaning on my face. The glass has fogged, but I can still see people’s faces through it. Blank, stunned faces. Some are surprised. Some could not care less. Some take the flyers from my hand and drop them a few steps away. A metaphor for our fate. At first they take us with excitement; when the excitement fades they throw us on the ground. I ask myself why I am standing here with a mask on my face. Why, instead of sitting at a desk in a large organization, am I handing out flyers in Vanak Square?

\***

It is the moral-education hour. First year of high school. The teacher talks about starting a magazine and cultural work, and that is enough for me to walk into the adventure without thinking. No background in making a magazine, or even in teamwork. The fruit was four different magazines over four years, with kids for whom free reading meant nothing. Every year I started with a team and finished the year alone. My inability to hold people’s attention and lead them was already obvious in elementary school, but I never got tired, and I never thought “leadership is not my work.” I gathered the pieces myself, tried to write through a thousand miseries so I would look like a hard worker. (My first real writing happened there, and of course it was terrible.) I laid out pages in Microsoft Publisher with a thousand miseries, and in 1383 I tried to sell each issue for two hundred tomans, just to cover the paper and not lose money.

I mixed in some childish tricks, like taking ads from students. It did not work. Nobody paid for an ad in the magazine.

In those four years I never managed to gather a motivated team. What remained were incomplete lessons in publishing, team-building, and selling.

My interest in IT started then. I thought I should be a flag-bearer for a new generation of seventies IT people. (I once wrote a note against the communications minister of the time and almost got thrown out of school.) You can read that note, and one of the second-year issues, [here](http://amirhp.ir/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/exir3.pdf).

## Dreaming of university

University was a world away from my dreams, but what I had helped me, in my second year, revive the scientific association’s magazine after four years. I wrote about Linux, the interest of those days, and tried to use pieces from [my blog](http://binazir.wordpress.com/). Once again I got stuck in a large part of layout and sales.

[![](http://amirhp.ir/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/IMG_02332.jpg)](http://amirhp.ir/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/IMG_02332.jpg)

The result of all this was that at twenty I walked into [Peivast](http://peivast.com/).

## Jumping into the deep end

I never forget my first day at Peivast, when [Arash](https://twitter.com/ArashBarahmand) handed me a stack of newspapers to read. I thought they were full of the usual unimportant trade news and PR from large companies. (A strange habit of mine: when I bought a magazine I read every page so the money would be *halal*. The habit started in elementary school with Doost magazine and lasted through pre-university, when I read Asr-e Ertebat. So I had a lot of information about things I did not like.) After a short skim I put them aside and told myself that was enough.

At 11:50, sitting in a corner with the sweet feeling that “I have done my assigned work,” Arash came over and said I had to go to an event that started at 12. I went white. I thought I would sit in a corner as before, browse the internet, and gather material like a researcher. Leaving the building, for someone who had barely moved around Tehran, was dread. Arash called it jumping into the deep end of the pool. It threw me into things that might otherwise have taken a long time to meet.

Calling important people and interviewing them at Peivast, for someone who feared the phone and thought it the most nauseating invention, helped me talk, make friends, call, and pull out what I needed. Those skills later saved me from serious trouble and pushed me toward many chances.

## A pure startup person

My interest in startups began when the idea arrived in Iran, in 1392. Working at Peivast at twenty, I met strange people who put themselves through a great deal of pain so they would not follow a current, they would *make* one. People who fought hard and sometimes lost everything they had. The first report I wrote at Peivast was about one of the most interesting of them, [Amir Vahooshi](http://amirhp.ir/blog/1392/06/28/%da%af%d9%81%d8%aa%da%af%d9%88-%d8%a8%d8%a7-%d8%a7%d9%85%db%8c%d8%b1-%d9%88%d9%87%d9%88%d8%b4%db%8c-%d8%a8%db%8c%d9%be%e2%80%8c%d8%aa%db%8c%d9%88%d9%86%d8%b2-%d8%aa%d8%a7-%d8%af%db%8c%d8%ac%db%8c/), who built BeepTunes, has small and large startups in Rahnama’s portfolio, and now works on his foreign investment firm. (Mr. Vahooshi, I apologize here that when you search your name, my blog is the first result.)

The idea of startups excited me more every day, until in Ordibehesht 1393, in the middle of one of those adventures, everything suddenly became real. For a withdrawn, quiet boy, going into the street to sell a product that did not exist was impossible. That is what happened at [Lean Startup Machine](http://leanstartupmachine.ir/).
