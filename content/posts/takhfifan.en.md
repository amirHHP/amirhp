I wrote the titles and the intro in 1397, but for various reasons I never finished it and it was never published. After this much time, the subject is probably no longer confidential.

By the time this is out, the news of Takhfifan and Netbarg merging for good will likely have landed, so I can share what I learned in nine months at Takhfifan more freely. The merger is ultimately good for everyone. Their exclusive postures only raised the cost of operating and marketing, and they produced no real profit for either side. Of course a business like this can barely work without exclusivity.

## As a founder, learn when to let go

Fighting and carrying on is not always a virtue. There are places where you have to leave things alone. You can treat it as the equivalent of [Let’s let fires burn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-best-entrepreneurs-let-fires-burn-reid-hoffman/), which Reid Hoffman talked about in one of his podcasts. If you cannot let a lot of things go, you will lose a lot of other things too.

Takhfifan’s founder could not (or would not) let go of anything. He followed the smallest things with the most granular actions, and he involved himself in the price math of every single sale.

## As a CEO, believe in delegation

If you cannot delegate, you can probably forget about scale, and if you do not scale you are nothing more than a personal or family business. Delegation requires trusting people. If you have a trust problem, or you are afraid to trust people and you constantly check them and assume they will slack off, you fall into [micromanagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micromanagement). It wears you out fast and it will not let you think bigger.

Takhfifan’s CEO delegated very little (in the sense of actually handing something to someone else). When he did, he did it in a way that was bound to fail so he could step back in. (This is a common trap: doing the work so that it proves you right.)

## Make internal communication paths efficient

If information does not move well inside the organization, you will hit trouble and messages will not land. These days tools like Teams, Slack, Click, or the Iranian Limo try to make internal communication cleaner.

Takhfifan used Telegram then. Sometimes things went by email. The problem was that it was not done well: there was a group for every subject.

## Look at the org chart again every year

The most important thing about an org structure is that it works. People should sit in places where they matter, and you should not be afraid to change it.

Takhfifan’s structure had slowly become meaningless. Supervisors were barely different from managers. Nobody knew the layers, and there was no reference chart. So a new hire spent a long time proving and discovering their place, and they did not perform well for a while.

## Do not talk badly about your people behind their backs

I think this is the worst possible thing: a CEO who does not trust the people he pays every day, and who uses any chance to tear them down.

My own experience: a week after I started I was hit with a pile of criticism. Why isn’t so-and-so doing anything? Why is their performance bad?

## Give people a reason to decide

People with no motive to decide, who fear the consequences, only take your time and hang around your neck. If you do not give them a reason to decide, why are you hiring anyone? I think only a small part of the organization should be purely executors. The rest should decide and move.

## Let them believe they have an effect

Your people have to believe they matter and that they can do large things. Otherwise they erode day by day and do less work that is worth anything.

## Do not praise without a target (have clear criteria for praising your people)

Aimless praise only confuses them. They no longer know what is good and what is bad, and over time they do things that are not desirable — things they were once praised for.

## Do not make working more, or working hard, a value. Smart work is the value

That your people work late into the night and on holidays is not a value. It means some process is broken, and it means you will soon be crushed under the gears and you will not be able to grow.

## Do not break your word. Be committed to what you say

This one thing can wreck the whole frame of an organization. Say something once and walk it back, and you lose all credit inside your own company forever. Nobody will count on your word or on the picture you draw.
