Preface: In this new series I want to write more, and more freely, about what I have lived. Breaking my old frames is hard, but I will try, unlike the newspaper and magazine writing I got used to, to come back to personal writing. “Ten reasons some people become entrepreneurs” is the first piece of this new chapter, and the plan is to publish something new every Saturday.

In the past year, which I spent entirely on starting [Mibarim](http://mibarim.com/), I thought a lot about what I gained and what I lost. In this piece I stretched that thought to a simpler question: why does an entrepreneur leave routine work for the hard work of entrepreneurship? I also tried, when hiring, to see what temperament brings people to our team and what temperament makes working with them impossible. I will write later about who is right for the first days of a startup. Right now I want to answer: why do some people become entrepreneurs?

## 1. Entrepreneurs enjoy creating

One of the first reasons people leave a safe employee job is that a strong wish to create overtakes everything else. They are tired of realizing other people’s dreams. They are willing to lose a lot to taste making something. In entrepreneurship you may have to do shallow work that has nothing to do with your expertise: designing a logo, finding a name, laying out the org, arranging the office, cleaning the workplace. An entrepreneur enjoys all of it because in the end it belongs to their own work and their own dream.

## 2. They like leading a team and an organization

Many people like leading a team and building one. That does not mean they inherently like giving orders, because mere management and commanding will knock a new entrepreneur over very fast. Their character is more guide than guided. They do not want a boss; they want to decide freely and show their creativity. (Even the best entrepreneurs still have to answer to others for their decisions.)

## 3. They want to get rich

Entrepreneurship is one of the hardest, riskiest paths to wealth. People are basically two kinds: those who understand risking for a large pile of capital, and those who prefer to get rich by stacking small amounts over years. The second is the routine employee life. Only some people succeed in getting large money through entrepreneurship. Some fail many times on the way, but what is clear is that at the end of the road there is, finally, money.

## 4. They suffer from a problem

The reason many startups exist (especially the successful ones) is a problem. If the entrepreneur is one of the people who suffered that problem, and they see their solution as the answer to an old pain, their odds are much higher, because they are already a customer and they understand the whole business. One reason someone starts something themselves and rolls up their sleeves is that they have given up on existing solutions. Traffic and air pollution were a large hassle for me. Solving that hassle mattered enough that I put part of my professional life into it.

## 5. Risk excites them

Entrepreneurs love risk. It raises their adrenaline sharply, even if it costs them everything they own. They strangely want their capital to either multiply overnight or be destroyed. In plain language they are not afraid of gambling. They like entering a market they know nothing about and dealing with customers they do not know at all.

## 6. They want freedom for their imagination to fly

A lot of bosses block your creativity. So people who want no limit on their creativity either have to find a good boss who lets them be (almost impossible) or go build their own work. Entrepreneurship needs a lot of creativity. In marketing, product, sales, support, people, and fundraising you need creativity as well as knowledge. In my last job it was very hard to put new ideas into practice. They usually got no attention or no support.

## 7. Other entrepreneurs matter to them

Many people admire entrepreneurs. The hardness and pain in an entrepreneur’s life is respectable, and that is why they turn to entrepreneurship themselves. Talking with experienced founders thrills them, and entrepreneurs are their first models. For me, people who bootstrapped their startups are deeply respectable, and I try to learn from them.

## 8. Daily work exhausts them badly

A lot of people are used to daily work. Every morning they go to the office and do yesterday’s work in the same frame. For many others that is a large torture. I personally prefer, when I arrive, to have no picture of how the day will go. Who will I see? Will everything from the past year change today? Wrestling with all that uncertainty is a pleasure. [Entrepreneurs get tired of sameness and routine very fast.](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/251838) That is why many of them leave even their own company once it becomes routine and mature, and get ready to create the next thing.

## 9. They want to learn at a high rate

Entrepreneurship lets you learn a lot of theory in practice. Much of what you learned in school, and much of what you learned in large companies, changes in a real environment full of innovation. In the year I worked on Mibarim I met ideas I had only on paper, tried them in real Iran, and saw how different they were from advice with no action.

## 10. A sense of getting there

When the work is entirely yours, you see every step of progress and every result. Every mistake takes you off the path; every right decision brings you one step closer. You never feel that, in a large company, when the main pressure is absorbed by the managers. At Mibarim I see the result of every decision directly. Every mistake is a large step toward failure, every right decision a tangible step toward success, and that is very valuable to me.
