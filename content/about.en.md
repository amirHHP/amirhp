In Shahrivar 1386 I said hello to blogging with the default post on [WordPress.com](http://wp.com/). For two years I was discovering myself and what I cared about. I was a teenager looking for the truth, and 1388 was the big shift in how I saw the world. The internet was almost cut off — though in 1398 I learned you can cut it **completely**. After my [WordPress blog](http://binazir.wordpress.com/) was shut down I wrote a little on [Blog.com](http://forfreeweb.blog.com/), then decided I would only write for [Linux Review](http://linuxreview.ir/author/amirhp/) and only about Linux. Six years after my first post online, I finally got this domain.

I moved some of the more important pieces I had written here and there onto this site. The full archive of my old blog is available [here](https://docs.google.com/document/d/15b_2QNmHm9GBrLDiDMULzM-5rfSAtlxFy8dzAzTHHg4/edit?usp=sharing).

[I spent three years as an IT journalist](/en/posts/startup-story-1-school-scape/). I wrote about the structure of Iranian tech companies before those structures had even formed. I wrote about Iranian startups when you could still count them on one hand, and about tools that made life a little easier. Later, in Salam Donya magazine, I tried a different kind of profile writing. [One event](/en/posts/startup-story-2-lean-startup/) changed my path: I jumped in and started my own startup. [At Mibarim I learned](/en/posts/startup-story-3-cofounder/) that nothing works if you never leave the circle of “yourself.” Mibarim did not go anywhere, but it was the start of a long road toward product management and starting a few more companies. After Mibarim I founded Nobaar, and at Takhfifan I learned what it means to be a good employee in a hundred-person company. In my last chapter I went to Alibaba to see how every equation changes once a company grows past three hundred people.

You can read my pieces in Peivast magazine [here](/old-peivast/).

Free software once mattered a great deal to me, because it was the story of people who refused to be victims of the defaults — people who lived a kind of utopia, unbound.

I write sometimes. I think I should write more. I don’t know why. But I have a feeling that, in the end, it is the writing. For me, writing is how you last. They say the medium has changed, that the audience has no patience, but I still think you can find everything in the turns of these texts.
